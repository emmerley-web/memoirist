About

Emily Liao

Her approach

Writing that listens before it speaks.

Emily Liao is a ghostwriter and memoirist. She has ghostwritten, edited, and consulted on more than ten books with clients across four continents.

Her background spans law, AI, and thought leadership — a combination that shapes how she approaches every story: with curiosity, rigour, and a conviction that the most interesting detail is usually the one people assume isn't worth mentioning.

She came to ghostwriting after leaving a tech startup in 2021. Her first project was a legacy memoir — she sent over a piece of short fiction with nothing to lose, got the gig, and spent months interviewing one of the most remarkable people she'd ever met. She's been doing it ever since.

Clients often say the finished book sounds more like them than anything they'd have written themselves.

Philosophy

On storytelling
and craft

Emily approaches every project with the same conviction: her job is to dig deep, unearth the rawness of experience that makes writing so human. That means asking "Why?" and "What did that mean to you?" far more than most people expect.

She specialises in business memoir — story-led, with lessons woven throughout the narrative. The best books in this genre do more than recount a career. They offer something the reader can carry with them.

She believes stories are small antidotes to loneliness. That's the real reason to write a book — not the achievement of it, but the connection.

Working closely with someone on their story takes trust on both sides. Emily takes that seriously.

Who she works with

The right
collaboration

Emily's clients come from all walks of life: CEOs, serial entrepreneurs, political refugees, trauma survivors, public figures, artists. What they share is a story worth telling and a genuine reason to tell it.

Her ideal collaborator is someone who cares more about providing meaningful wisdom to the reader than satisfying their own ego — and who is committed to the process of deep collaboration, creative input, and iterative refinement. The book has to matter to them.

If you're unsure whether your story qualifies, it almost certainly does. The question is whether you're ready to tell it.
